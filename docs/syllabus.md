# WAEC SSCE Physics — topic scope

Condensed from the WAEC syllabus "NOTES" column. These are the boundaries for each lesson's
learning goals. Questions are set in SI units.

## Part I — Interaction of matter, space & time

1. **Concepts of matter**: simple structure of matter; solid, liquid, gas; particle evidence (Brownian motion); kinetic theory used to explain states, gas pressure, evaporation and boiling, cohesion, adhesion, capillarity; crystalline vs amorphous solids (face-centred, body-centred).
2. **Fundamental and derived quantities**: length, mass, time, current, luminous intensity, temperature, amount of substance (m, kg, s, A, cd, K, mol); volume, density, speed (m³, kg m⁻³, m s⁻¹).
3. **Position, distance and displacement**: X, Y, Z coordinates; measuring with string, metre rule, vernier calipers, micrometer screw gauge (accuracy); bearings with compass and protractor; distance vs displacement, located graphically.
4. **Mass and weight**: lever, beam and spring balances, plus digital balances; kg vs N.
5. **Time**: time as the interval between events; heartbeat, sand clock, ticker-timer, pendulum, stopwatch; the second.
6. **Fluids at rest**: volume, density, relative density (solids and liquids); pressure and Pascal's principle (hydraulic press, brakes); pressure vs depth; atmospheric pressure; barometer, manometer, siphon, syringe, pump; U-tube and Hare's apparatus; Archimedes' principle; law of flotation (hydrometer, balloons, ships, submarines).
7. **Motion**: types of motion (qualitative); relative motion (co-linear numericals); force as cause; contact vs field forces; static and dynamic friction, coefficient of limiting friction, its pros, cons and reduction; viscosity and terminal velocity; circular motion (angular speed vs velocity, centripetal force, banking of roads).
8. **Speed and velocity**: uniform and non-uniform; ticker-timer; v = Δs/Δt; distance/displacement–time graphs and instantaneous speed.
9. **Rectilinear acceleration**: a = Δv/Δt; ticker-timer; velocity–time graphs (acceleration and displacement); equations of motion; free fall.
10. **Scalars and vectors**: examples; representation; addition (force board); resolution; resultant velocity analytically and graphically.
11. **Equilibrium of forces**: moments, couples (tap, corkscrew, steering); resultant and equilibrant; parallelogram and triangle of forces; equilibrium conditions for parallel and non-parallel forces; centre of gravity and stability (stable, unstable, neutral).
12. **Simple harmonic motion**: loaded test tube, pendulum, spring, bifilar suspension; linear vs angular speed and acceleration; period, frequency, amplitude; g from a pendulum or spring (no derivation); energy of SHM; forced vibration and resonance.
13. **Newton's laws**: inertia, mass and weight; F = ma (ticker-timer); momentum and impulse; conservation of momentum, elastic collisions in a line; recoil, jet and rocket propulsion; action and reaction.

## Part II — Energy: mechanical and heat

14. **Energy**: forms of energy; renewable and non-renewable resources; conservation of energy and transformations.
15. **Work, energy and power**: joule, kWh; work in a gravitational field; deriving PE and KE; conservation of mechanical energy; power in watts; machines (levers, pulleys, inclined plane, wedge, screw, wheel and axle, gears) with MA, VR and efficiency; the bicycle; friction in machines.
16. **Heat energy**: temperature and thermometers (liquid-in-glass, max/min, clinical, constant-volume gas, resistance, thermocouple, pyrometer); Celsius and Kelvin; effects of heat; linear, area and volume expansivity (bridges, bimetallic strips, thermostats, sagging cables, buckling rails), real vs apparent expansion, anomalous expansion of water; conduction, convection, radiation (land and sea breezes, vacuum flask); gas laws (and air bags); heat capacity and specific heat capacity (method of mixtures, electrical method); latent heat, melting and boiling points (impurities, pressure, pressure cooker); evaporation; vapour pressure (SVP and boiling); humidity, dew point, wet- and dry-bulb hygrometer; dew, fog, rain.

## Part III — Waves

17. **Production and propagation of waves**: ropes and slinky; ripple tank; amplitude, wavelength, frequency, period; v = fλ, T = 1/f.
18. **Types of waves**: transverse vs longitudinal; y = A sin(ωt ± 2πx/λ) explained (no phase-difference questions).
19. **Properties of waves**: reflection, refraction, diffraction, interference, superposition and standing waves (ripple tank, plane and circular waves).
20. **Light waves**: sources; luminous and non-luminous bodies; rectilinear propagation (shadows, eclipse, pinhole camera); plane mirrors (laws, images, inclined mirrors, rotation, periscope, sextant, kaleidoscope); curved mirrors (1/u + 1/v = 1/f, m = v/u, focal length, uses); refraction (real and apparent depth, critical angle, TIR, lateral displacement, deviation, minimum deviation μ = sin((A+Dₘ)/2)/sin(A/2), optical fibres, mirage); lenses (formula, magnification, power in dioptres); optical instruments (camera vs eye, defects, microscopes, telescopes, projector, binoculars); dispersion and colour mixing.
21. **Electromagnetic waves**: radio, infrared, visible, UV, X-rays, gamma (descriptions and uses).
22. **Sound waves**: sources; a medium is needed; speed in solids, liquids and gases (temperature and pressure); echoes and reverberation (ocean depth, exploration); pitch, loudness, quality; sonometer, f = (1/2l)√(T/m); forced vibration, resonance, overtones; open and closed pipes, resonance tube, end correction.

## Part IV — Fields

23. **Fields**: gravitational, electric and magnetic fields; field lines (compass, iron filings).
24. **Gravitational field**: g = F/m; Newton's law of gravitation, G vs g; gravitational potential; escape velocity.
25. **Electric field**: charging by friction, induction and contact; electroscope; charge distribution; lightning conductors; field lines; Coulomb's law and permittivity; field intensity and potential; capacitance (parallel plate, series and parallel, stored energy, uses); cells (simple, Daniell, Leclanché, lead-acid, alkaline); Ohm's law; series and parallel circuits, lost volts, internal resistance; ohmic vs non-ohmic; electrical energy and power, heating effect, motors, solar cells; shunts and multipliers; resistivity; ammeter, voltmeter, potentiometer, Wheatstone and metre bridge.
26. **Magnetic field**: magnetic materials (soft iron vs steel); magnetisation and demagnetisation; flux (Wb) and flux density (T); fields of a magnet, wire and solenoid; neutral points; force on a conductor (motor, galvanometer) and between parallel conductors; electromagnets (bell, earpiece); Earth's field (dip, declination); F = qvB sin θ.
27. **Electromagnetic field**: Fleming's left-hand rule; Faraday's and Lenz's laws; generators (a.c. and d.c.), E = E₀ sin ωt; induction coil, transformer; self and mutual inductance (henry, E = ½LI²); eddy currents; power transmission and household wiring.
28. **Simple a.c. circuits**: I = I₀ sin ωt graphs; peak and r.m.s.; phase in R, L and C; series RLC; X_L, X_C, impedance; vector diagrams; resonance (radio and TV tuning); a.c. power.

## Part V — Atomic and nuclear physics

29. **Structure of the atom**: Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr and electron-cloud models and their limits; energy levels, Franck–Hertz, line, absorption and discharge spectra; photoelectric effect, work function, threshold frequency, Einstein's equation; thermionic emission; X-rays (tube, properties, uses, hazards).
30. **Structure of the nucleus**: A, Z, N; nuclides, isotopes; α, β, γ; G–M counter; half-life and decay constant; transmutation; uses of radioactivity; fission vs fusion, binding energy, mass defect, E = Δmc²; reactors, safety.
31. **Wave–particle paradox**: electron diffraction; duality of matter and light.

## Harmonised short-structured-question topics

Dimensional analysis; projectiles; satellites and rockets; Hooke's law and Young's modulus;
thermal conductivity, solar collectors, black-body radiation; fibre optics; LASER; magnetic
materials; band theory, semiconductors, p–n junction, rectification; diffusion; wave–particle paradox.
